# Final Project Proposal Submission

Prepare the proposal for your Federated Edge Intelligence System capstone project.

## Overview

Your proposal is the foundation for your capstone project: designing and implementing a complete **Federated Edge Intelligence System**. This proposal will guide your work for the remainder of the semester and must demonstrate both technical depth and practical feasibility.

**For this proposal, the project is individual by default, but teams of up to 4 are acceptable with instructor approval.**

If you choose a team:

- Submit one proposal with all member names and student IDs.
- Add a **Team Plan & Roles** subsection outlining contributions, division of work, and coordination.
- Scope should scale with team size, such as a larger architecture, richer evaluation, or additional advanced features.
- Clearly attribute any external resources or collaborations.

## Required Proposal Sections

### 1. Problem Statement and Motivation — 0.5–1 page

- Clearly define the real-world problem you are addressing.
- Explain why federated learning is the appropriate solution.
- Discuss the significance and impact of your chosen application.
- Identify your target federated participants and their constraints.

### 2. System Architecture Design — 1–1.5 pages

- Present a high-level system architecture diagram.
- Describe the federated learning topology: centralized, decentralized, hierarchical, or hybrid.
- Specify device types, capabilities, and heterogeneity considerations.
- Outline communication protocols, aggregation flow, and data flow.
- Address privacy and security architectural decisions.

### 3. Technical Approach — 1–1.5 pages

- Detail your federated learning algorithm and aggregation strategy.
- Specify privacy preservation techniques, such as differential privacy, secure aggregation, or privacy-aware update filtering.
- Describe hardware-aware optimization strategies.
- Outline your model compression and deployment pipeline.
- Identify 2–3 advanced features you plan to implement.

### 4. Implementation Plan — 0.5–1 page

- Choose between real hardware deployment or comprehensive simulation.
- Specify programming languages, frameworks, libraries, and tools.
- Provide a realistic timeline with milestones.
- Identify potential risks and mitigation strategies.
- Describe your development and testing environment.

### 5. Evaluation Methodology — 0.5 page

- Define success metrics for your system.
- Specify performance benchmarks and baselines.
- Describe privacy and security evaluation methods.
- Plan for scalability, efficiency, communication, and compression analysis.

## Project Options

Choose one of the following options or propose your own. Option 5 requires instructor approval during the proposal phase.

### 🦌 Option 1: Federated Wildlife Conservation

**Participants:** Camera traps, acoustic nodes, ranger phones, park-zone gateways, conservation cloud server.  
**Challenges:** Intermittent connectivity, battery-powered sensors, non-IID habitat data, sensitive animal-location information.  
**Example Task:** Animal species recognition, empty-frame detection, or poaching-risk event classification.

### 🌱 Option 2: Federated Smart Agriculture

**Participants:** Farmer phones, greenhouse cameras, soil sensors, farm gateways, agricultural cooperative server.  
**Challenges:** Rural connectivity, different crop types, different climates, non-IID farm data, limited edge hardware.  
**Example Task:** Crop disease classification, pest-risk prediction, or crop-stress detection.

### 🎓 Option 3: Federated Personalized Education

**Participants:** Student tablets or laptops, classroom hubs, school edge servers, district-level aggregator.  
**Challenges:** Sensitive student data, heterogeneous devices, different learning patterns, fairness, client dropout.  
**Example Task:** Student mastery prediction, disengagement-risk detection, or personalized difficulty recommendation.

### ⚡ Option 4: Federated Home Energy Optimization

**Participants:** Smart meters, thermostats, home energy hubs, neighborhood aggregators, utility server.  
**Challenges:** Household privacy, time-series forecasting, device heterogeneity, bandwidth limits, asynchronous participation.  
**Example Task:** Next-hour energy demand forecasting, peak-load prediction, or battery-use recommendation.

### 💡 Option 5: Your Own Idea

Propose your own federated learning application. It must demonstrate equivalent technical complexity and real-world relevance. Your idea must include heterogeneous edge clients, a clear federated learning task, privacy or security concerns, hardware-aware optimization, and measurable evaluation metrics. **Instructor approval is required during the proposal phase.**
